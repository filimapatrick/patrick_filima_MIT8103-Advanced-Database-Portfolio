### Executive Summary

Database normalisation is the formal mathematical process of decomposing complex relational schemas into smaller, well-structured relations to eliminate data redundancy and mitigate operational anomalies (Codd, 1970; Date, 2004). In multi-site neuroimaging research, data routinely originate from disparate MRI consoles, clinical electronic health records (EHR), and automated compute pipelines. Without rigorous normalisation, storing such multi-modal information in monolithic or poorly structured tables leads to severe data anomalies, compromised patient privacy, and loss of referential integrity.

This report documents the step-by-step normalisation of the African Neuroimaging Research Data Management System (ANRDMS) from an **Unnormalised Form (UNF)** flat acquisition log through **First Normal Form (1NF)**, **Second Normal Form (2NF)**, **Third Normal Form (3NF)**, and **Boyce-Codd Normal Form (BCNF)**.

### 1. The Baseline: Unnormalised Form (UNF)

In typical multi-site research environments, imaging facilities often record experimental sessions using flat spreadsheets or comma-separated log files generated by acquisition consoles. 

Consider the hypothetical unnormalised relation:

$$\text{UNF\_SCAN\_LOG}(\underline{\text{ScanLogID}}, \text{HospitalName}, \text{HospitalCity}, \text{ScannerModel}, \text{FieldStrength}, \text{ResearcherName}, \text{ResearcherEmail}, \text{ParticipantID}, \text{BirthYear}, \text{Sex}, \{\text{DiagnosisCode}, \text{ConditionName}, \text{Severity}\}, \{\text{SequenceType}, \text{TR}, \text{TE}, \text{VoxelSize}, \text{BIDSPath}\}, \{\text{PipelineName}, \text{JobStatus}, \text{ExitCode}\})$$

Where attributes enclosed in braces $\{\dots\}$ represent repeating groups (multi-valued attributes for a single scan session).

#### Concrete Example of UNF Anomaly-Prone Record:
* **ScanLogID:** `LOG-2025-001`
* **Hospital:** `Lagos University Teaching Hospital`, `Idi-Araba, Lagos`
* **Scanner:** `Siemens MAGNETOM Skyra`, `3.0T`
* **Researcher:** `Patrick Filima`, `p.filima@anrdms.research.ng`
* **Participant:** `ANRDMS-LOS-001`, `1968`, `Female`
* **Diagnoses (Repeating):** `[DX-STR-001: Ischemic Stroke, Moderate], [DX-HYP-001: Hypertension, Mild]`
* **Scans (Repeating):** `[T1w, TR=2300, TE=2.98, 1.0mm, sub-01_T1w.nii.gz], [FLAIR, TR=9000, TE=85, 3.0mm, sub-01_FLAIR.nii.gz]`
* **Pipelines (Repeating):** `[FreeSurfer 7.4.1, COMPLETED, 0], [BIANCA 1.2.0, COMPLETED, 0]`

#### Operational Anomalies in UNF:
1. **Insertion Anomaly:** A newly installed 3.0T MRI scanner at National Hospital Abuja cannot be registered in the database until an actual research participant undergoes a scan session. Similarly, a clinical condition (e.g., *Early-Onset Alzheimer's*) cannot exist in the database without being diagnosed in an active patient.
2. **Deletion Anomaly:** If participant `ANRDMS-LOS-005` withdraws ethical consent and their single scan log is deleted, all historical technical specifications regarding the scanner calibration and the diagnosing researcher are permanently lost.
3. **Update (Modification) Anomaly:** If Lagos University Teaching Hospital updates its official contact email or hospital tier, this fact must be redundantly updated across hundreds of thousands of historical scan rows. Any incomplete update causes inconsistent institutional metadata.

### 2. First Normal Form (1NF)

#### Definition:
A relation $R$ is in First Normal Form (1NF) if and only if all underlying domains contain only atomic (indivisible) values, and there are no repeating groups or multi-valued attributes (Elmasri & Navathe, 2015).

#### Transformation:
To convert $\text{UNF}$ to $\text{1NF}$:
1. Flatten all repeating groups by expanding multi-valued collections into distinct tuples.
2. Define a composite primary key that uniquely identifies each tuple.

$$\text{FLAT\_1NF}(\underline{\text{ParticipantID}, \text{ScanID}, \text{DiagnosisCode}, \text{PipelineID}}, \text{HospitalName}, \text{City}, \text{State}, \text{ScannerID}, \text{FieldStrength}, \text{ResearcherEmail}, \text{BirthYear}, \text{Sex}, \text{ConditionName}, \text{Severity}, \text{SequenceType}, \text{TR}, \text{TE}, \text{BIDSPath}, \text{PipelineName}, \text{JobStatus})$$

#### Functional Dependency Set ($F$):
* $FD_1$: $\text{ParticipantID} \to \text{BirthYear}, \text{Sex}, \text{HospitalName}, \text{City}, \text{State}$
* $FD_2$: $\text{ScannerID} \to \text{FieldStrength}, \text{HospitalName}$
* $FD_3$: $\text{DiagnosisCode} \to \text{ConditionName}$
* $FD_4$: $\text{ScanID} \to \text{ParticipantID}, \text{ScannerID}, \text{SequenceType}, \text{TR}, \text{TE}, \text{BIDSPath}$
* $FD_5$: $\text{PipelineID} \to \text{PipelineName}$
* $FD_6$: $\text{ScanID}, \text{PipelineID} \to \text{JobStatus}$
* $FD_7$: $\text{ParticipantID}, \text{DiagnosisCode} \to \text{Severity}$

#### Evaluation:
Although the relation is in 1NF (all values are atomic), it exhibits catastrophic redundancy. Non-key attributes are partially dependent on parts of the composite candidate key $(\text{ParticipantID}, \text{ScanID}, \text{DiagnosisCode}, \text{PipelineID})$.

### 3. Second Normal Form (2NF)

#### Definition:
A relation is in Second Normal Form (2NF) if it is in 1NF and every non-prime attribute is **fully functionally dependent** on the primary key (no partial functional dependencies). 

A partial dependency occurs when a non-prime attribute depends on a proper subset of a composite primary key:
$$X \subset PK \implies X \to Y \quad (\text{Partial Dependency})$$

#### Decomposition:
We decompose $\text{FLAT\_1NF}$ into distinct relations corresponding to the determinants of the partial dependencies:

1. **`PARTICIPANTS_2NF`** (Determinant: $\text{ParticipantID}$)
   $$\underline{\text{ParticipantID}} \to \text{BirthYear}, \text{Sex}, \text{SiteID}, \text{HospitalName}, \text{City}, \text{State}$$
2. **`DIAGNOSES_2NF`** (Determinant: $\text{DiagnosisCode}$)
   $$\underline{\text{DiagnosisCode}} \to \text{ConditionName}, \text{Category}$$
3. **`PARTICIPANT_DIAGNOSES_2NF`** (Determinant: $\text{ParticipantID}, \text{DiagnosisCode}$)
   $$\underline{\text{ParticipantID}, \text{DiagnosisCode}} \to \text{Severity}, \text{DiagnosisDate}$$
4. **`MRI_SCANS_2NF`** (Determinant: $\text{ScanID}$)
   $$\underline{\text{ScanID}} \to \text{ParticipantID}, \text{ScannerID}, \text{FieldStrength}, \text{SequenceType}, \text{TR}, \text{TE}, \text{BIDSPath}$$
5. **`PIPELINES_2NF`** (Determinant: $\text{PipelineID}$)
   $$\underline{\text{PipelineID}} \to \text{PipelineName}, \text{Version}$$
6. **`PROCESSING_JOBS_2NF`** (Determinant: $\text{ScanID}, \text{PipelineID}$)
   $$\underline{\text{ScanID}, \text{PipelineID}} \to \text{JobStatus}, \text{ExitCode}$$

#### Evaluation:
Partial dependencies are completely resolved. However, transitive dependencies still remain.

### 4. Third Normal Form (3NF)

#### Definition:
A relation $R$ is in Third Normal Form (3NF) if it is in 2NF and **no non-prime attribute is transitively dependent on the primary key**.

Formally, for every functional dependency $X \to Y$ in $R$:
1. $X \to Y$ is trivial ($Y \subseteq X$), OR
2. $X$ is a superkey of $R$, OR
3. Every attribute in $Y \setminus X$ is a prime attribute (part of a candidate key).

#### Identifying Transitive Dependencies in 2NF Relations:
In `PARTICIPANTS_2NF`:
$$\text{ParticipantID} \to \text{SiteID} \quad \text{and} \quad \text{SiteID} \to \text{HospitalName}, \text{City}, \text{State}$$
Here, $\text{SiteID} \to \text{HospitalName}$ is a transitive dependency where $\text{SiteID}$ is not a superkey of `PARTICIPANTS_2NF`.

In `MRI_SCANS_2NF`:
$$\text{ScanID} \to \text{ScannerID} \quad \text{and} \quad \text{ScannerID} \to \text{FieldStrength}, \text{Manufacturer}, \text{SiteID}$$
Here, scanner hardware attributes transitively depend on $\text{ScanID}$ via $\text{ScannerID}$.

#### 3NF Decomposition:
To achieve 3NF, transitive dependencies are isolated into dedicated master entity tables:

1. **`research_sites`**:
   $$\underline{\text{site\_id}} \to \text{hospital\_name}, \text{city}, \text{state}, \text{tier}, \text{contact\_email}$$
2. **`mri_scanners`**:
   $$\underline{\text{scanner\_id}} \to \text{site\_id}, \text{manufacturer}, \text{model\_name}, \text{magnetic\_field\_strength\_tesla}, \text{gradient\_strength\_mt\_m}$$
3. **`participants`**:
   $$\underline{\text{participant\_id}} \to \text{primary\_site\_id}, \text{birth\_year}, \text{biological\_sex}, \text{handedness}, \text{consent\_obtained}$$
4. **`mri_scans`**:
   $$\underline{\text{scan\_id}} \to \text{participant\_id}, \text{scanner\_id}, \text{project\_id}, \text{sequence\_type}, \text{tr}, \text{te}, \text{dicom\_uid}, \text{bids\_path}$$
5. **`neurological_diagnoses`**:
   $$\underline{\text{diagnosis\_code}} \to \text{condition\_name}, \text{category}, \text{icd11\_code}$$
6. **`participant_diagnoses`**:
   $$\underline{\text{participant\_diagnosis\_id}} \to \text{participant\_id}, \text{diagnosis\_code}, \text{researcher\_id}, \text{severity\_stage}$$
7. **`processing_pipelines`**:
   $$\underline{\text{pipeline\_id}} \to \text{name}, \text{version}, \text{container\_image}, \text{target\_sequence}$$
8. **`processing_jobs`**:
   $$\underline{\text{job\_id}} \to \text{scan\_id}, \text{pipeline\_id}, \text{submitted\_by\_id}, \text{status}, \text{exit\_code}$$

### 5. Boyce-Codd Normal Form (BCNF)

#### Definition:
A relation $R$ is in Boyce-Codd Normal Form (BCNF) if and only if for every non-trivial functional dependency $X \to Y$, **$X$ is a superkey** (Codd, 1974). 

BCNF is a stricter version of 3NF that eliminates anomalies arising when a relation has multiple overlapping candidate keys.

#### Verification of BCNF Compliance in ANRDMS:

Let us examine the candidate keys and determinants for the core relations:

1. **Relation `mri_scans`:**
   * Candidate Keys:
     1. $K_1 = \{\text{scan\_id}\}$
     2. $K_2 = \{\text{dicom\_series\_uid}\}$
     3. $K_3 = \{\text{bids\_relative\_path}\}$
   * Functional Dependencies:
     * $\text{scan\_id} \to \text{participant\_id}, \text{scanner\_id}, \text{acquisition\_date}, \dots$ (Determinant is superkey $K_1$)
     * $\text{dicom\_series\_uid} \to \text{scan\_id}, \dots$ (Determinant is superkey $K_2$)
     * $\text{bids\_relative\_path} \to \text{scan\_id}, \dots$ (Determinant is superkey $K_3$)
   * **Result:** All determinants are superkeys. $\therefore$ `mri_scans` is in **BCNF**.

2. **Relation `researchers`:**
   * Candidate Keys:
     1. $K_1 = \{\text{researcher\_id}\}$
     2. $K_2 = \{\text{email}\}$
   * Functional Dependencies:
     * $\text{researcher\_id} \to \text{full\_name}, \text{email}, \text{role}$ (Determinant is superkey $K_1$)
     * $\text{email} \to \text{researcher\_id}, \text{full\_name}, \text{role}$ (Determinant is superkey $K_2$)
   * **Result:** All determinants are superkeys. $\therefore$ `researchers` is in **BCNF**.

3. **Relation `participant_diagnoses`:**
   * Candidate Keys:
     1. $K_1 = \{\text{participant\_diagnosis\_id}\}$
     2. $K_2 = \{\text{participant\_id}, \text{diagnosis\_code}\}$
   * Functional Dependencies:
     * $\text{participant\_diagnosis\_id} \to \text{participant\_id}, \text{diagnosis\_code}, \text{severity\_stage}$ (Determinant is $K_1$)
     * $\{\text{participant\_id}, \text{diagnosis\_code}\} \to \text{severity\_stage}, \text{diagnosis\_date}$ (Determinant is $K_2$)
   * **Result:** Every determinant is a candidate key / superkey. $\therefore$ `participant_diagnoses` is in **BCNF**.

### 6. Mathematical Proof: Lossless-Join & Dependency Preservation

To ensure the decomposition of $\text{FLAT\_1NF}$ into the set of normalized relations $\mathcal{D} = \{R_1, R_2, \dots, R_k\}$ is mathematically sound, it must satisfy two fundamental theorems:

#### Theorem 1: Lossless-Join Guarantee
A decomposition $R = (R_1, R_2)$ with functional dependencies $F$ is lossless with respect to $F$ if and only if the common attributes contain a candidate key for at least one of the decomposed relations:
$$R_1 \cap R_2 \to R_1 \quad \text{OR} \quad R_1 \cap R_2 \to R_2$$

*Proof for `mri_scanners` and `research_sites`:*
* $R_1 = \text{mri\_scanners}(\underline{\text{scanner\_id}}, \text{site\_id}, \text{manufacturer}, \text{field\_strength})$
* $R_2 = \text{research\_sites}(\underline{\text{site\_id}}, \text{hospital\_name}, \text{city}, \text{state})$
* $R_1 \cap R_2 = \{\text{site\_id}\}$
* Since $\text{site\_id}$ is the primary key of $R_2$, the functional dependency $\text{site\_id} \to R_2$ holds.
* Therefore, the natural join $R_1 \bowtie R_2$ reproduces the original relation without introducing spurious tuples. $\quad \blacksquare$

#### Theorem 2: Dependency Preservation
A decomposition $\mathcal{D} = \{R_1, R_2, \dots, R_k\}$ preserves dependencies if:
$$\left( \bigcup_{i=1}^k \pi_{R_i}(F) \right)^+ = F^+$$
Where $\pi_{R_i}(F)$ denotes the projection of functional dependencies onto relation $R_i$.
*In our schema, every functional dependency $X \to Y \in F$ involves attributes that appear together in at least one decomposed relation schema $R_i$. Enforcing primary key and unique constraints locally in PostgreSQL is sufficient to ensure global integrity without requiring multi-table cross-assertion triggers.* $\quad \blacksquare$

### 7. Normalisation vs. Denormalisation Trade-offs

While BCNF eliminates storage redundancy and guarantees update consistency, real-world neuroinformatics platforms often encounter trade-offs between transactional purity (OLTP) and analytical performance (OLAP):

| Architectural Dimension | Normalized Relational Schema (3NF / BCNF) | Denormalized Analytical / Document Model (NoSQL) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Write Performance** | Exceptional: Inserts, updates, and deletes touch exactly one record without side-effects. | Moderate to Poor: Updates require modifying duplicate occurrences across many records. |
| **Storage Footprint** | Minimal: No duplicated string attributes or redundant specifications. | Higher: Redundant embedded schemas, key strings, and metadata. |
| **Query Complexity** | Requires multi-table `JOIN` operations (e.g. 5-table join to aggregate scan counts by hospital). | High read throughput: Single-read document or flattened table retrieval. |
| **Best-fit in ANRDMS** | Core clinical registry, participant enrollment, ethical consent, scan provenance, job tracking. | Image metadata cataloging, BIDS sidecar JSON parsing, interactive web visualization (Portfolio 4). |
